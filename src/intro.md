# Welcome to Earthmania

**Earthmania** is a living field guide to sustainability and to how the everyday
materials and systems around us actually work — the bottle in your hand, the
packaging in your bin, the infrastructure that carries it all away.

This is **not** an academic text. The goal is clear, practical explanations that
anyone can read in a few minutes: what something is, how it's made, where it ends
up, and what the real trade-offs are. Pages are written to be skimmed, revisited,
and improved over time.

## How to use this guide

Each entry follows the same structure, so you always know where to look:

1. **Overview** — what it is, in plain language
2. **How It's Made** — raw inputs and key processes
3. **Common Uses** — where you meet it day to day
4. **Where It Accumulates** — where it goes once it leaves our hands
5. **Recycling & End-of-Life** — what realistically happens to it
6. **Trade-offs & Alternatives** — honest comparisons, no greenwashing
7. **Sources & Attribution** — where the information came from

## Open-source synthesis

Earthmania is built in the open. Its pages synthesise publicly available research,
government and industry data, and openly licensed material into readable summaries.
Every claim should be traceable to a listed source, and reused material is credited
using the TASL format (Title, Author, Source, License).

Spotted an error or want to add something? Use the edit icon at the top of any page
to suggest a change on [GitHub](https://github.com/buerlino/earthmania).
